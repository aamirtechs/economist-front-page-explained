2026 is still being written. The year-ahead cover mixed celebration with danger, and by the autumn the magazine was asking the question every investor was asking: what happens when the AI money party slows down?

---

## "The World Ahead 2026": annual preview issue, published November 2025

**What the cover shows:** A round globe drawn in black, white and red, packed with dozens of small icons: world leaders, the Kremlin, tanks, missiles, warships and rockets, syringes and pills, a birthday cake with "250" on it for America's 250th year, a footballer for the 2026 World Cup, burning forests, wind turbines and refugee tents.

**What was happening:** The issue looked ahead to a year holding America's 250th birthday and a World Cup in North America, while wars, the AI race and trade fights were still running.

**The point it made:** The magazine said 2026 would be a year of big celebrations on the surface and deep danger underneath, with geopolitics drifting in a risky direction.

**Verdict: Partly true.** The big set pieces on the cover did happen in 2026: America marked its 250th birthday in July and the World Cup was played in North America in the summer. Wars and the AI race also continued, as the darker icons suggested.

## "The sorcerer of silicon": September 2026

**What the cover shows:** An illustrated portrait of Jensen Huang, the boss of chip giant Nvidia, holding a glowing computer chip like a magic object. He is presented as a wizard whose chips power the whole AI boom. (The exact week of this issue could not be verified from open sources.)

**What was happening:** Nvidia had become one of the most valuable companies on Earth because every AI firm needs its chips. Huang was treated like the central figure of the AI age.

**The point it made:** The cover profiled Huang and Nvidia at the very top of the AI boom, and asked how long one company could hold so much power over a world-changing technology.

**Verdict: Not a prediction.** It was a portrait of a man at his peak, not a forecast. Still, traders noticed what happened next: Nvidia's shares fell about 29 percent in the following weeks, which fed the old market joke about a "magazine cover curse".

## "Whatever next?": November 14, 2026

**What the cover shows:** Giant metallic letters spelling "AI", patterned with circuits, are cracking open at the bottom. Green banknotes and red wires pour out of the broken letters, and a small man in a suit tumbles backwards in the falling money.

**What was happening:** After years of giant spending on AI, investors had started asking whether AI companies could ever earn back the money poured into them. Nvidia's shares had fallen hard since September, and the stock market debut of AI firm Anthropic was being watched as a test of the whole boom.

**The point it made:** The magazine asked whether the AI money party is ending: whether AI firms can make profits that match the huge sums invested in them.

**Verdict: Too early to tell.** The issue is only days old. The honest answer is that the question on the cover, what happens to AI money next, is still open.
