# 2014

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

2014 mixed the old world and the new: Scotland voting on its future, an oil price war that changed energy for good, and a cover about the startup explosion that got the next decade right.

---

## "A Cambrian moment": January 18, 2014

**What the cover shows:** The cover carried a special report on tech startups. The name compares startups to the Cambrian explosion of new life forms about 540 million years ago. (A reliable description of this cover's artwork could not be confirmed from open sources, so this entry does not guess at it.)

**What was happening:** Cheap cloud computing, smartphones, app stores and ready made online services had made it very cheap to start a tech company. Startups were appearing everywhere, and Uber, Airbnb and similar firms were starting to shake old industries.

**The point it made:** An explosion of digital startups would reshape whole industries and even change what a company is. In the report's famous last line, "today's digital primordial soup contains the makings of the economy of tomorrow."

**Verdict: Came true.** In the ten years after, startups and tech platforms transformed transport, hotels, shops, media, money and work itself, and the number of billion dollar startups, called unicorns, grew from a handful to well over a thousand.

*Sources: [Global Development Studies paper](https://www.globaldevelopmentstudies.com/_files/ugd/700e71_d514385365134176ab189f518e54cc47.pdf), [Internet Retailing](http://internetretailing.net/opinion-retail-medias-cambrian-moment/)*

## "UK RIP?": September 2014

**What the cover shows:** The Union Jack, the flag of the United Kingdom, drooping sadly on its flagpole, like a flag at a funeral. The picture asks whether the 300 year old union of Scotland and England is about to die.

**What was happening:** Scotland was days away from voting on independence, and one poll had just put the Yes side ahead, which shocked London. The result was too close to call. The referendum took place on September 18, 2014.

**The point it made:** The magazine argued that leaving the union would be a mistake for Scotland and a tragedy for the country it left behind, and it urged Scots to vote No.

**Verdict: Not a prediction.** It was an argument, and the side the magazine backed won: Scotland voted No to independence by 55 percent to 45 percent, so the United Kingdom survived.

*Source: [FCDO blog](https://blogs.fcdo.gov.uk/petermillett/2014/10/01/scotland-the-wise/amp/)*

## "Sheikhs v shale": December 6, 2014

**Cover line:** "Sheikhs v shale", with the subline "The new economics of oil".

**What the cover shows:** A Saudi sheikh and an American shale oilman standing back to back like two duelists in an old western film, both frowning, holding petrol pump nozzles at the ready like pistols. The Economist itself described this cover again in a 2024 article looking back at the oil price war.

**What was happening:** The oil price had crashed from about 115 dollars a barrel in June 2014 to about 70 dollars. American shale drillers had flooded the market with new oil, and Saudi Arabia and OPEC refused to cut their own output to save the price.

**The point it made:** The fight between the sheikhs and the shale drillers had tipped the world from oil shortage to oil surplus. The economics of oil had changed for good, and prices would now be set by a real market fight.

**Verdict: Came true.** Oil fell below 30 dollars a barrel in early 2016 and many shale firms went bust, but the shale industry survived, got cheaper, and made the United States the world's biggest oil producer. OPEC was forced to team up with Russia in a new group, OPEC+, in 2016, which proved the old order had changed.

*Sources: [Archive.org scan of the issue](https://ia801201.us.archive.org/21/items/The_Economist_6TH_December-12TH_December_2014/The_Economist_6TH_December-12TH_December_2014.pdf), [Qatar News App](http://www.qatarnewsapp.com/2/Article/2028/238058659)*
