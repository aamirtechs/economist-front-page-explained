## "The charming outcome of the Cancún trade talks": September 20, 2003

**What the cover shows:** A desert at sunset in Mexico. In front stands a big green cactus shaped exactly like a human hand giving the middle finger, the rude sign, to the reader. The polite headline is meant as a joke.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The cactus: the classic plant of the Mexican desert, placing the picture exactly where the talks were held, in Cancún.
- The cactus shaped like a hand: it turns a desert plant into a human gesture, so the landscape itself seems to speak.
- The raised middle finger: the rude sign meaning "go away", which is what the deadlocked talks were, in effect, saying to the world.
- The word "charming" above it: polite British understatement placed next to a rude picture is the whole joke, because the outcome was the opposite of charming.
- The empty sunset desert: a dry place where nothing grows, matching talks that produced nothing.

**What was happening:** Trade ministers from around the world met in Cancún, Mexico, for World Trade Organization talks meant to open up world trade. Poor and rich countries could not agree, mainly about farm subsidies, and the talks collapsed with no deal.

**The point it made:** The polite word "charming" next to a rude gesture was the magazine's sarcastic way of saying the talks had failed badly and ended in insult and deadlock.

**Verdict: Not a prediction.** It was a comment on a failure that had already happened. The Doha round of trade talks that Cancún belonged to was never finished and stayed stuck for years; the WTO reached only a much smaller deal in Bali in 2013.

**What happened next:**
- September 2003: the Cancún meeting broke up with no deal, with poorer countries blaming rich country farm subsidies.
- 2004 to 2012: the Doha round stayed stuck, and meeting after meeting failed to finish it.
- December 2013: WTO members agreed the smaller Bali package, the first worldwide trade deal in the WTO's history.
- Later years: the Doha round was quietly left unfinished, and trade deals moved to smaller regional agreements instead.
