In 2006 the magazine gave climate change the full front page treatment, with a special report arguing that uncertainty was a reason to act, not a reason to wait.

---

## "The heat is on": September 9, 2006

**What the cover shows:** A photograph of a dry, parched desert under a hot sky. The only sign of life in the empty landscape is a single tough cactus.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The parched desert: dry land where almost nothing can live is the simplest possible picture of a hotter world.
- The hot, empty sky: no clouds and no rain in sight, so no relief is coming.
- The single cactus: a cactus survives where other plants die, a small sign that only the toughest life can cope with more heat.
- A real photograph, not a cartoon: using an actual landscape makes the warning feel like news, not a story.

**What was happening:** The science of climate change was becoming hard to deny, and the magazine published a long special report on it. Scientists expected the world to warm by between 1.4 and 5.8 degrees Celsius during this century, and a major British report on the cost of climate change, the Stern Review, was about to appear.

**The point it made:** The leader argued: "The uncertainty surrounding climate change argues for action, not inaction. America should lead the way." In plain words, not being fully sure is a reason to act now, not a reason to wait.

**Verdict: Partly true.** The warming warning proved right: the world is now about 1.2 degrees hotter than in pre-industrial times and extreme heat has grown. But America did not lead in a steady way: it joined the Paris climate deal in 2015, left it in 2020, and joined again in 2021.

**What happened next:**
- October 2006: the Stern Review, the British report mentioned above, warned that ignoring climate change could badly damage the world economy.
- December 2015: nearly 200 countries signed the Paris Agreement to limit global warming.
- 2020: America left the Paris deal under President Trump, then rejoined in 2021 under President Biden.
- 2020s: record heatwaves, floods and wildfires hit many countries, matching the cover's warning about a hotter world.
