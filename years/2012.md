# 2012

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

In 2012 the euro crisis reached its peak. The covers mocked Scotland's independence plans, warned about France twice, and asked whether Germany might walk away from the euro. History gave mixed marks.

---

## "Skintland": April 14, 2012

**Cover line:** "It'll cost you", for the cover story "The price of Scottish independence". The map itself carries the famous word "Skintland".

**What the cover shows:** A joke map of Scotland renamed "Skintland", which means a land with no money. Edinburgh becomes "Edinborrow", Glasgow becomes "Glasgone", the Highlands become the "Highinterestlands", Oban becomes "Obankrupt" and Inverness becomes "Inamess".

**What was happening:** Scotland's government, led by Alex Salmond, was planning the 2014 independence referendum. The big fight was about money: oil income, debts, and whether Scotland could afford to go it alone.

**The point it made:** Independence would come at a high price and could leave Scotland as one of Europe's weak and vulnerable small economies.

**Verdict: Partly true.** Scots voted No in 2014, so the warning was never tested directly. But the cover's worry about oil money looked strong soon after, when the oil price crashed in 2014 to 2016 and the oil income in the Yes campaign's plans collapsed. Salmond was furious at the time and said the magazine would "rue the day" it ran the cover.

*Sources: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2012/4/17/scottish-independence-wholl-pay-the-price), [Public Finance](https://www.publicfinance.co.uk/2012/04/skintland-slur-shakes-up-scots)*

## "The rather dangerous Monsieur Hollande": April 28, 2012

**What the cover shows:** The cover puts François Hollande, the Socialist candidate for president of France, on the front with the warning headline across him. It treated a mild looking politician as a danger to Europe.

**What was happening:** Hollande was about to win the French presidential election, promising a 75 percent tax on the rich, a lower retirement age for some workers, and a fight against austerity in Europe.

**The point it made:** Hollande's plans were dangerous for France's economy and for the euro. France needed reform, not more spending and taxes.

**Verdict: Partly true.** Hollande won and did push through the 75 percent tax idea, business confidence fell and unemployment rose, and he ended his term deeply unpopular. But he did not blow up the euro, he dropped or softened his most radical plans, and he later turned more friendly to business.

*Source: [Wall Street Pit](https://wallstreetpit.com/91551-turning-the-tide-on-austerity/)*

## "Tempted, Angela?": August 11, 2012

**What the cover shows:** Angela Merkel sitting at a table with a bottle of liquor and a half empty glass beside her, studying a secret document. The document is titled "How to break up the euro", as if Germany's leader was quietly thinking about ending the single currency.

**What was happening:** The euro crisis was at its peak. Spain and Italy were in trouble, many people expected Greece to be pushed out of the euro, and all eyes were on Germany, the country paying for the rescues.

**The point it made:** The magazine asked whether Germany might get tired of paying and let the euro break apart.

**Verdict: Did not happen.** Days before this cover, the European Central Bank chief Mario Draghi had promised to do "whatever it takes" to save the euro, and Merkel backed keeping Greece inside. The euro survived, and no country has ever left it.

*Source: [Forex Factory news](https://www.forexfactory.com/news/378500-germany-considers-holding-eu-referendum/comment/5921643)*

## "The time-bomb at the heart of Europe": November 17, 2012

**What the cover shows:** Seven French baguette loaves tied together with a ribbon in the blue, white and red colours of the French flag, with a lit fuse sparking from the middle. The baguettes are arranged like a bundle of dynamite sticks. The cover story carried the "time-bomb" title.

**What was happening:** The euro crisis had calmed in Spain and Italy, and attention turned to France: high public spending, rising debt, weak growth and a new president, François Hollande, who was moving slowly on reforms.

**The point it made:** France, not Greece or Spain, could become the biggest danger to the euro, because its economy was weak and its reforms were too small.

**Verdict: Did not happen.** French ministers reacted with fury and called the cover sensationalist, and in the end the markets never attacked France. French borrowing costs stayed low, growth stayed slow, and there was no explosion, though France's high debt kept growing.

*Sources: [Hurriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/french-officials-slam-the-economist-cover-story-34803), [Emirates 24/7](https://www.emirates247.com/news/region/france-furious-at-economist-time-bomb-taunt-2012-11-16-1.483427)*
