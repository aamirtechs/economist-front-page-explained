# 2011

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

2011 was the year of the Arab Spring and the euro crisis at its worst. It also gave the magazine one of its rudest and most famous covers, aimed at Italy's leader.

## "Just as the world economy was recovering": March 5, 2011

**What the cover shows:** Planet Earth seen from space, with a black oil barrel marked "OIL" sitting on top of North Africa and the Middle East. A lit fuse sparks on top of the barrel, like a bomb about to blow up the recovery.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- Planet Earth from space: the whole world economy is the patient in this picture.
- The black oil barrel: it sits exactly on North Africa and the Middle East, the region that supplies the oil.
- The lit fuse: the barrel is drawn as a bomb, and the fuse is already burning, so time is short.
- The title's timing joke: trouble arrives "just as" the world was getting better, which makes it feel crueller.

**What was happening:** The Arab Spring uprisings had spread to Libya, which was sliding into civil war, and the oil price had jumped above 100 dollars a barrel. The world economy was still fragile after the financial crisis.

**The point it made:** Trouble in the oil producing region could push oil prices so high that it would kill the young world recovery.

**Verdict: Partly true.** Oil stayed expensive through 2011 and the euro crisis badly hurt growth in Europe. But the world economy still grew by about 4 percent that year, so the recovery bent without breaking.

**What happened next:**
- 2011: Libya slid into civil war, and its oil output collapsed for a time.
- Through 2011: oil stayed expensive, above 100 dollars a barrel.
- 2011 to 2012: the euro crisis badly hurt growth across Europe.
- End of 2011: the world economy had still grown by about 4 percent that year, so the recovery bent without breaking.

## "The man who screwed an entire country": June 11, 2011

**What the cover shows:** A simple, bold photograph of Italy's prime minister Silvio Berlusconi smiling and beaming at the camera. There is no cartoon and no trick, just his grin under one of the rudest headlines the magazine has ever printed. This was the European edition cover.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The plain photograph: no cartoon and no trick, just his real face, which makes the attack feel direct and personal.
- The big grin: he beams at the camera, pleased with himself, while the headline above him condemns him.
- The rude headline: one of the harshest lines the magazine has ever printed, chosen to shock.
- The empty background: nothing distracts the eye. It is just the man and the charge against him.

**What was happening:** Italy had barely grown in ten years under Berlusconi, who was also fighting court cases and sex scandals. The euro debt crisis was starting to reach Italy, a country too big to rescue easily.

**The point it made:** This was a judgement, not a prediction. The magazine argued that Berlusconi's years in power had badly damaged Italy, leaving it with almost no growth, huge debts and weak institutions.

**Verdict: Not a prediction.** Five months later, in November 2011, Berlusconi was forced to resign when Italy's borrowing costs shot up. Italy's economy stayed weak for many years afterwards, which kept the cover's judgement in the news.

**What happened next:**
- November 2011: Italy's borrowing costs shot up, and Berlusconi lost his majority in parliament.
- November 12, 2011: Berlusconi resigned as prime minister, five months after this cover.
- November 2011: Mario Monti took over at the head of a government of experts.
- After 2011: Italy's economy stayed weak for years, and Berlusconi never became prime minister again.

## "That's all, folks!": November 12, 2011

**What the cover shows:** Berlusconi placed inside a classical painting of lazy rulers from the last days of the Roman Empire, as if his time in power had been one long cartoon show that was now ending. The headline copies the famous ending line of old Looney Tunes cartoons.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The classical painting: Berlusconi sits among the lazy rulers of the late Roman Empire, linking his Italy to an empire in decline.
- The relaxed, idle mood: the rulers lounge around enjoying power while the state sinks into debt.
- The headline, "That's all, folks!": the famous last line of Looney Tunes cartoons, so his rule is mocked as one long cartoon show.
- The double edge of the joke: it is funny, but it also says a whole era, not just one man, has finished.

**What was happening:** Italy's debt crisis had exploded, markets no longer trusted Italy, and Berlusconi had lost his majority in parliament. He resigned on November 12, 2011.

**The point it made:** The Berlusconi era, which had lasted on and off for 17 years, was finally over.

**Verdict: Came true.** Berlusconi resigned that same week and a government of experts led by Mario Monti took over. Berlusconi stayed in politics for years, but he never became prime minister again.

**What happened next:**
- November 12, 2011: Berlusconi resigned on the very day this issue was dated.
- November 2011: Mario Monti formed a government of experts to calm the markets and cut the debt.
- February 2013: Italy held an election, and elected politicians took over again from Monti's team.
- Later years: Berlusconi stayed a force in Italian politics, but he never returned as prime minister.
