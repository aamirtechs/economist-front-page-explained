# 2025

2025 belonged to one man. The year-ahead cover said Donald Trump would dominate the world, and the April cover answered his tariff war with one of the sharpest images of the year.

---

## "The World Ahead 2025": annual preview issue, published November 2024

**What the cover shows:** Donald Trump stands at the centre of the cover, surrounded by symbols of what was coming with him: trade wars, rockets and nuclear risk, surveillance, dollar signs and rising charts.

**What was happening:** Trump had just won the US election in November 2024 and was preparing to return to the White House. He was promising big tariffs, tax cuts and a hard line on immigration.

**The point it made:** The magazine said one man, Trump, would dominate world politics in 2025, and that his trade wars and unpredictable style would shake the whole world.

**Verdict: Came true.** Trump took office in January 2025 and dominated the year: sweeping tariffs, clashes with allies, deportations and constant headline-making decisions, exactly the Trump-centred year the cover promised.

## "Ruination Day": April 5, 2025

**Cover line:** "Ruination Day", with the sub-line "How to limit the global damage".

**What the cover shows:** On a bright yellow background, Donald Trump bends over and saws through a green map of the United States with a handsaw. He is cutting into the very country he leads. The headline twists Trump's own name for his tariff day, "Liberation Day", into "Ruination Day".

**What was happening:** On April 2, 2025, Trump announced huge new tariffs on almost every country in the world. Stock markets around the world crashed within days, and businesses feared a global recession.

**The point it made:** The magazine called the tariffs mindless and said they would cause economic havoc in America and the world. Its leader argued the damage should be limited as much as possible.

**Verdict: Partly true.** The first part came true fast: markets plunged and trade was badly disrupted. But Trump then paused or softened many of the tariffs, so the full global recession the cover feared did not happen, even though trade stayed costlier and more uncertain.
