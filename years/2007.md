# 2007

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

2007 was the year the financial crisis began quietly, in bank lending, months before the public noticed. Both covers below saw the danger early, and both were right.

---

## "Lessons from the credit crunch": October 20, 2007

**Cover line:** "Lessons from the credit crunch: A special report on central banks and the world economy".

**What the cover shows:** A big pink piggy bank stands on dry, cracked earth, like land in a drought. Far behind it stand two much smaller piggy banks, one blue and one orange.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The big pink piggy bank: a piggy bank stands for savings and for banks, the places where money is meant to sit safely.
- The dry, cracked earth: land in a drought stands for credit drying up, because banks had stopped lending to each other.
- The two smaller piggy banks in the distance: the trouble is not one bank's alone, the whole herd is standing on the same cracked ground.
- A child's toy in a dead landscape: something innocent and cheerful placed in a disaster scene makes the point that risky lending had been made to look safe.

**What was happening:** In the summer of 2007 the American housing bust had frozen lending between banks, in what was called the credit crunch. In Britain, savers had queued to take their money out of the Northern Rock bank, and central banks were pumping money into the system.

**The point it made:** The special report, called "Only Human", said central banks had kept interest rates too low for too long, which fed the housing and credit boom. It argued they should watch asset prices and credit booms, not just normal inflation, to stop the next bust.

**Verdict: Came true.** The crunch was only the start. In 2008 the problem became the worst world financial crisis since the 1930s: big banks failed or had to be rescued, and central banks cut interest rates to near zero and created money on a huge scale.

**What happened next:**
- September 2007: savers had already queued outside the British bank Northern Rock, the first run on a British bank in over a century.
- September 2008: Lehman Brothers collapsed, and the credit crunch became a global financial crisis.
- 2008 to 2009: central banks cut interest rates to near zero and created new money to keep the banking system alive.
- 2010s: tougher new rules forced banks to hold more capital, putting into practice the kind of lesson this cover had asked for.

*Sources: [Instagram archive post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DTKMBz6DAct/), [Legal Anthology](https://www.legalanthology.ch/t/cottier_challenges-ahead_2009.pdf)*

## "America's vulnerable economy": November 17, 2007

**What the cover shows:** A copy of the famous film poster for "Jaws". A woman swims happily on the surface of the sea, wearing a stars and stripes bikini, while a huge monster shark rises from the deep water beneath her, mouth open.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The happy swimmer: she stands for American families and shoppers, enjoying life and spending freely.
- The stars and stripes bikini: the flag pattern makes clear that the swimmer is America itself.
- The giant shark rising from below: the shark is the hidden danger of debt and weak banks, which the swimmer cannot see.
- The copy of the "Jaws" film poster: borrowing a famous film image tells the story at a glance, a calm surface, a monster underneath, and an attack on the way.

**What was happening:** By late 2007 the credit crunch was spreading, house prices were falling, the dollar was weak, and oil was near 100 dollars a barrel. Many people still hoped America would avoid a recession.

**The point it made:** The cover said the American economy looked calm on the surface, but big dangers were lurking just below it, above all debt, falling house prices, and troubled banks.

**Verdict: Came true.** Only weeks later the American economy slid into what became the Great Recession, officially dated from December 2007. In September 2008 the hidden danger broke the surface and the crisis spread across the world.

**What happened next:**
- December 2007: the American recession officially began, only weeks after this cover appeared.
- September 2008: Lehman Brothers collapsed, and the danger under the surface broke into the open, much as the cover had pictured.
- 2008 to 2009: the crisis spread around the world, and millions of people lost their jobs and homes.
- June 2009: the Great Recession officially ended in America, though the recovery that followed took years.
