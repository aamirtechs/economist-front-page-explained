# 2000

The century opened with a cover about Africa that the magazine itself later reversed. It is one of the most debated covers in its history, and a good lesson in how a confident front page can age badly.




## At a glance

| Cover | Date | Theme | The call | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The hopeless continent | May 13, 2000 | Politics and leaders | Africa's new century had brought more disaster than hope | ❌ DID NOT HAPPEN |

## "The hopeless continent": May 13, 2000

**What the cover shows:** A map of Africa fills the cover. Inside the shape of the continent is a photo of a young African fighter with a rocket launcher resting on his shoulder, straining under its weight. The headline sits above the map.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The map shape: Africa itself is the frame of the picture, so the judgement is about a whole continent, not one country.
- The young fighter inside the map: war is made the face of Africa, as if fighting is all the continent does.
- The rocket launcher on his shoulder: a heavy weapon of war, and he strains under its weight, hinting that the wars crush ordinary life.
- The word "hopeless" above the map: the headline delivers its verdict before the reader opens a single page.

**What was happening:** The year 2000 had started badly for Africa, with floods in Mozambique, famine returning in Ethiopia, violence in Zimbabwe, and a brutal civil war in Sierra Leone. Many outsiders saw the whole continent as a place of war, hunger, and disease.

**The point it made:** The magazine said Africa's problems were mostly man made, not bad luck, and that the new century had brought the continent more disaster than hope. It asked, in effect, whether the world should give up on Africa.

**Verdict: Did not happen.** The judgement proved far too gloomy. Africa's economies grew strongly through the 2000s, and in December 2011 The Economist itself published the opposite cover, "Africa rising".

**What happened next:**
- 2001 to 2010: Africa's economies grew strongly for a decade, helped by high prices for oil and raw materials and by new investment from China.
- December 2011: The Economist published "Africa rising", the mirror image of this cover.
- Later years: the 2000 cover kept being quoted in debates about how the media judges Africa, usually as a warning against writing off a whole continent.
