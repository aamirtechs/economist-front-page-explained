## "Remember": September 7, 2002

**What the cover shows:** The Twin Towers stand alone against a flaming orange sunset sky. It is a quiet, sad picture of the towers as they looked before they were destroyed, with the single word "Remember" as the headline.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The towers standing whole again: the photo shows them alive, as a memory, not as news.
- The flaming orange sunset: a sunset stands for something ending, and the warm colours make the picture sad rather than angry.
- The towers alone in the frame: with nothing else shown, they stand for all the people who were lost, not just two buildings.
- One single word, "Remember": it works as both an order and a promise, like words carved on a war memorial.

**What was happening:** It was the first anniversary of the September 11 attacks. America had already driven the Taliban from power in Afghanistan, and President Bush was building the case for war against Iraq.

**The point it made:** The leader line was "September 11th changed the world. But not enough." The magazine argued that the world had not yet done enough to stop terror and dangerous weapons.

**Verdict: Not a prediction.** It was an anniversary argument. Within six months America did invade Iraq, in March 2003, largely in the name of that fight, and the "war on terror" then ran for 20 years, until the pullout from Afghanistan in 2021.

**What happened next:**
- September 2002: memorial events were held across America on the first anniversary of the attacks.
- March 2003: America invaded Iraq, a war argued for partly in the name of September 11.
- September 2011: the national September 11 memorial opened in New York on the site where the towers had stood.
- August 2021: American troops finally left Afghanistan, ending the twenty year war that followed the attacks.

## "Preparing for terror": November 30, 2002

**What the cover shows:** The Earth is drawn as a human skull. The map of the world is drawn on the skull in red, like blood, making the planet look like a warning sign of death.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The Earth drawn as a skull: the planet itself is a dead head, saying the danger is everywhere, not in one country.
- The world map on the skull: the continents are still easy to recognise, so every reader sees their own home on the skull.
- The red colour, like blood: it turns the map into a wound and ties the picture to violence.
- A skull as a warning sign: like the skull printed on a bottle of poison, it tells people to prepare before harm comes, which is the cover's whole message.

**What was happening:** Terror attacks were spreading beyond America, with the Bali nightclub bombing in October 2002 and an attack on a hotel and an Israeli plane in Kenya in November 2002. Governments were spending heavily on security but attacks kept coming.

**The point it made:** The magazine said the world had become a dangerous place and that no one should relax. Terror was now global, and countries and ordinary people had to prepare for more attacks, not just react after them.

**Verdict: Came true.** Sadly, the warning held. Big attacks followed in Madrid in 2004, London in 2005, and Mumbai in 2008, and terrorism stayed near the top of the world agenda for the next two decades.

**What happened next:**
- March 2004: bombs on trains in Madrid killed nearly 200 people.
- July 2005: bombs on trains and a bus in London killed 52 people.
- November 2008: gunmen attacked hotels and a railway station in Mumbai, killing more than 160 people.
- 2010s: new terror groups rose in the Middle East and Africa, and attacks in European cities kept security at the top of politics.
