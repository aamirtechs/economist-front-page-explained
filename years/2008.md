# 2008

2008 was the year of the great financial crash. The Economist's covers followed the fall, from a slow American economy to a world standing on the edge of disaster.

## "The great American slowdown": April 12, 2008

**What the cover shows:** A snail crawling slowly, with the flag of the United States painted on its shell. The joke is simple: America, the engine of the world economy, had slowed to a snail's pace.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The snail: the classic symbol of slowness, so the American economy is moving at a crawl.
- The US flag on the shell: the slow animal is America itself, not just any economy.
- The shell as a house: a snail carries its home on its back, a quiet hint at the housing trouble weighing America down.
- The slow, plain crawl: nothing dramatic happens in the picture, which is the point. A slowdown creeps rather than crashes.

**What was happening:** American house prices were falling, banks were losing money on bad home loans, and the United States was quietly slipping into recession. The big question was whether the rest of the world could keep growing without America.

**The point it made:** America's slowdown would hurt the whole world, not just America. The magazine argued the world economy was still tied to the American consumer.

**Verdict: Came true.** The American recession turned into the global financial crisis of 2008 and 2009. World trade collapsed, and even fast growing countries like China were hit hard, so the world did not escape America's problems.

**What happened next:**
- September 2008: the bank Lehman Brothers collapsed, and the slowdown became a full financial crisis.
- Late 2008: stock markets and world trade fell sharply as the crisis spread to almost every country.
- June 2009: the American recession officially ended, but joblessness stayed high for years after.
- 2009: China and other fast growing economies slowed too, proving the world was still tied to America.

## "World on the edge": October 4, 2008

**What the cover shows:** One lone man standing at the edge of a crumbling cliff, looking down into a black empty space below him. The ground under his feet is breaking away, which makes the danger feel very close and very real.

**The picture, piece by piece:**
- The lone man: he stands for everyone at once, investors, workers and families, all facing the crisis.
- The crumbling cliff edge: the ground of the financial system is breaking away under his feet.
- The black empty space below: nobody can see how far the fall goes, and nothing is there to catch him.
- The man still standing, not yet falling: the cover warns that disaster is close, not that it has already happened.

**What was happening:** The bank Lehman Brothers had just collapsed, banks had stopped lending to each other, and stock markets were crashing around the world. Governments were rushing out rescue plans to stop a total collapse.

**The point it made:** The financial system was close to disaster, and the rich world was on the edge of a nasty recession. Even with bailouts, jobs would be lost and economies would shrink.

**Verdict: Came true.** The rich world fell into the Great Recession, the worst downturn since the 1930s. Unemployment rose sharply, just as the cover warned, though government rescues stopped the banking system from fully collapsing.

**What happened next:**
- October 2008: governments around the world rushed out bank rescue plans and guarantees.
- 2008 to 2009: the rich world fell into the Great Recession, the worst downturn since the 1930s.
- 2009: unemployment rose sharply across America and Europe, just as the cover warned.
- After 2009: the rescues stopped the banks from fully collapsing, but the recovery took years.
