# 2010

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

In 2010 the crisis moved to Europe. Greece could not pay its bills, and the covers asked whether the euro itself would survive. One quiet cover about Egypt turned out to be one of the best predictions the magazine ever made.

---

## "Acropolis now": May 1, 2010

**Cover line:** "Acropolis now", with the subline "Europe's debt crisis spins out of control" and the words "The horror, the horror".

**What the cover shows:** A copy of the film poster for "Apocalypse Now". Rescue helicopters marked EU and IMF fly over the Acropolis in Athens at sunset, and German leader Angela Merkel stands in front wearing army jungle clothes with a grim face, ready for a hard rescue mission.

**What was happening:** Greece had admitted its budget numbers were far worse than it had said, and it could no longer borrow money on the markets. Europe and the IMF were preparing a giant rescue loan.

**The point it made:** Greece was small, but its debt crisis could spread like a fire to the rest of Europe and threaten the euro itself.

**Verdict: Came true.** The crisis spread to Ireland, Portugal, Spain, Italy and Cyprus over the next two years. Greece needed three rescue packages, and its economy shrank by about a quarter, one of the worst falls ever in peacetime.

*Sources: [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20100506/if-they-can-do-it-cant-we), [Taylor and Francis chapter](https://uat.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9781351297561-9/acropolis-scott-macdonald-andrew-novo?context=ubx&refId=4f429442-db86-4287-9099-8b74e212144e)*

## "Shifting sands": July 2010

**What the cover shows:** Egypt's president, Hosni Mubarak, slowly sinking and disappearing into a desert sand dune. Only part of him is still visible above the sand.

**What was happening:** Mubarak had ruled Egypt for almost 30 years, he was old and ill, and nobody knew who would follow him. Under the surface, anger about prices, corruption and police abuse was growing.

**The point it made:** Change was coming to Egypt and the Arab world, even if it looked calm. Mubarak's grip on power was weaker than it seemed.

**Verdict: Came true.** This cover is now famous inside The Economist itself, because it ran about six months before the Arab Spring began. Mubarak was swept from power by huge street protests in February 2011.

*Source: [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news-detail-222976)*
