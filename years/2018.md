# 2018

[Back to the full index](../README.md)

In 2018 the public mood turned against the giant tech firms, and economists started asking when the long recovery from 2008 would end. Both covers below are still quoted today.

---

## "How to tame the tech titans": January 20, 2018

**What the cover shows:** Google, Facebook and Amazon are drawn as giant robots standing over the Earth. The robots are eating smaller robots and tearing shops and buildings out of the ground. They look like monsters from a film, far bigger than everything around them.

**What was happening:** The big tech firms had become hugely rich and powerful, and public opinion was turning against them after fake news, election interference stories and worries about addiction and privacy. The magazine called them "BAADD": big, anti-competitive, addictive and destructive to democracy.

**The point it made:** The firms had become so big that they were no longer just competing in the market, they were the market. The magazine said governments should use competition law more strongly and give people real rights over their own data, rather than simply breaking the firms up.

**Verdict: Partly true.** Governments did move. Europe brought in strict data rules (GDPR) in May 2018, fined Google billions, and later passed the Digital Markets Act in 2022. America opened big court cases against Google and Facebook in 2020. But no titan was broken up, and the companies kept growing richer.

*Sources: [WilmerHale, Antitrust Law Journal](https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/documents/20201230-antitrust-law-journal_antitrust-populism-and-the-consumer-welfare-standard-what-are-we-actually-debating.pdf), [Milled newsletter archive](https://milled.com/the-economist-ie/how-to-tame-the-tech-titans-XpHlDJA4w0nk2E4S)*

## "The next recession": October 13, 2018

**Cover line:** "The next recession", with the subheading "How bad will it be?"

**What the cover shows:** A tiny man is climbing up a steep red line, like a climber on a mountain. The line is a rising market chart. He is just reaching the top, and right in front of him the line falls off a cliff into the dark.

**What was happening:** The world economy had grown well in 2017, but in 2018 stock markets were falling, trade fights were growing, and the last recession, the great crash of 2008, was ten years in the past. Many people asked when the next one would come.

**The point it made:** The magazine said the next recession would probably not be as terrible as 2008, but rich countries were badly prepared for it. Interest rates were already so low that central banks had little room to cut them, and countries were too divided to work together.

**Verdict: Partly true.** A recession did come, in 2020, but for a reason nobody on this cover saw: the COVID-19 pandemic. It was the deepest world recession since the 1930s, though also very short. Central banks did find tools, cutting rates to zero and printing money on a huge scale, together with big government spending.

*Sources: [Langba blog archive](https://langba113.github.io/2018/10/13/Oct-13th-2018/), [Tutor2u Economics](https://www.tutor2u.net/economics/blog/preparing-for-the-next-recession)*
