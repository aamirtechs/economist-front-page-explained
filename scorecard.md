# The Predictions Scorecard

[Back to the full index](README.md)

Every cover in this project gets a verdict. This page collects the clearest results: the covers that called the future right, the covers that got it wrong, and the famous in-between cases people still argue about.

Verdicts used in this project:

- **Came true:** events broadly matched what the cover said or warned.
- **Partly true:** part of it happened, part of it did not, or it happened for a different reason.
- **Did not happen:** events went the other way.
- **Not a prediction:** the cover was a judgment, a portrait, or a report, not a forecast.
- **Too early to tell:** the story is still open.

## The covers that got it wrong

| Cover | Year | What it said | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| [The hopeless continent](years/2000.md) | 2000 | Africa had more disaster than hope | Africa grew strongly all decade; the magazine reversed itself with "Africa rising" in 2011 |
| [Resign, Rumsfeld](years/2004.md) | 2004 | The US defence secretary had to go over Abu Ghraib | He kept his job for two and a half more years |
| [The incompetent or the incoherent?](years/2004.md) | 2004 | The magazine backed Kerry over Bush | Bush won the 2004 election |
| [Tempted, Angela?](years/2012.md) | 2012 | Germany might let the euro break up | Draghi promised "whatever it takes", the euro survived intact |
| [The time-bomb at the heart of Europe](years/2012.md) | 2012 | France was the euro's biggest danger | Markets never attacked France, borrowing costs stayed low |
| [It could happen](years/2017.md) | 2017 | America and North Korea could blunder into nuclear war | The two leaders met three times instead, and the threats calmed |

## The famous in-between cases

- **[Brazil takes off](years/2009.md) (2009).** The rocket Christ cover became the classic example of the "magazine cover curse": the boom peaked, then Brazil crashed. The magazine later mocked its own cover with ["Has Brazil blown it?"](years/2013.md) in 2013.
- **[The Great Fall of China](years/2015.md) (2015).** The crash fear was real for a few months, but no Chinese or world crisis followed. The cover is now used in business schools as a famous wrong call.
- **[The next recession](years/2018.md) (2018).** A recession did come in 2020, but from a virus, not from any cause the cover discussed. Right answer, wrong reason.
- **[The coming food catastrophe](years/2022.md) (2022).** Food prices hit record highs and hunger rose, but a grain deal and falling prices avoided the worst famine the cover feared.

## Beware of fake covers

Two famous "Economist covers" were never published at all. Do not let them fool you:

- **Putin riding a panda.** This image, shared widely in March 2022, was only a rejected draft design for the "alternative world order" issue. The published cover shows the faces of Putin and Xi melting into one. (Fact check: Annie Lab.)
- **"The World Ahead 2027".** A detailed year-ahead cover shared on social media is AI generated and fake. The Economist confirmed this to AAP FactCheck.

If a dramatic cover looks too perfect, check it against the magazine's official cover archive before sharing it.
