# The Predictions Board

[Back to the full index](README.md)

Every cover in this project, on one page. On the left, what the cover predicted or said. On the right, the status: did it happen, is it still pending, or is it a question for the future?

**The score so far, from 62 covers checked:** ✅ 21 happened · 🟡 25 partly happened · ❌ 6 did not happen · ⚪ 9 were not predictions · ⏳ 1 still pending

## How to read the status column

| Status | What it means |
|---|---|
| ✅ HAPPENED | Events matched what the cover said or warned |
| 🟡 PARTLY HAPPENED | Part of it happened, or it happened for a different reason |
| ❌ DID NOT HAPPEN | Events went the other way |
| ⚪ NOT A PREDICTION | A judgment, a portrait or a report, not a forecast |
| ⏳ PENDING | Too early to tell. The story belongs to the future |

Two patterns stand out. The magazine is at its best on slow moving dangers: debt, housing bubbles, oil and trade. It is at its weakest on sudden political drama, where its hopes and fears often run ahead of events. And its single most famous habit is the cover curse: a cover story that lands exactly at the top of a boom, like "Brazil takes off" in 2009.

## The five covers to read first

1. **[After the fall](years/2005.md)** (2005): the housing bubble warning, three years early and exactly right.
2. **[Shifting sands](years/2010.md)** (2010): the sinking Mubarak cover that saw the Arab Spring coming.
3. **[The wordless Ukraine flag cover](years/2022.md)** (2022): no headline at all, and the most powerful cover of the decade.
4. **[Slowbalisation](years/2019.md)** (2019): the snail that named a whole era of world trade.
5. **[How bad will it get?](years/2020.md)** (2020): the first COVID cover, asking the question the whole world was about to live.
